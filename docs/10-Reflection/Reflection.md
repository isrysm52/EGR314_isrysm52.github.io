## Review of Module's Sucess

The mudule hit most hardware requirements when created. The schematic was hastily done before it was origionally sent, but during PCB creation it was fixed up and worked as intended afterwards. The one part that the module failed was the up and down 8-pin connectors. The PCB footnote was to small and the Rx and Tx placements were not class standardized. This was an oversight on my part and I should have seen and fixed it, but seeing that there were only two people in the wireless controller and we had matching Rx and Tx we decided that it was okay and we went forward with the difference because at that point, we already had our PCBs. 

## Startup Tip

1. I think that the most important thing to do is research and read datasheets for sensors. The main project I had problems with was the hall effect sensor. Half of the problem could have been avoided if I had started with reading the datasheet. 
2. I also think I should have read requirements more thouroughly and after each assignment. Even if you read the requirements, you will forget a few as you continue your project. Going back to them periodically could have helped save tiome and stress when finding parts of my project that were not up to standard. 

## Lessons Learned

1. Take your time to breathe when statring an assignment. If you rush this work, the next set of work will be rushed as well. 
2. Go back through your Github after every assignment to keep it up to date. This made the final report a cakewalk especially when you have finals around the corner. 
3. Read ahead for projects. If you don't you'll slowly get behind
4. Communicate with your teammates. Each person has their specialty, if you are struggling, they may not be. 
5. Do not use AI to make your code. I saw a whole bunch of people do this last semester and when it came time to change the code they were clueless on how their code even worked. Use it to help you not do everything for you.
6. Take time to know your classmates. They are there to help you and you should be there to help them. 
7. Time management is key when you want to get everything done. For most people, this is not their only class, but this will be a class that takes up most of your time. You should prepare for that while you are not in class. This is an easy class to fall behind in. 
8. The datasheets are there to guide you along your struggles. Most problems that I had could be solved when reading the data sheet and seeing that my pins were mistyped. 
9. Choose a project that you'll have fun with. The coding feels a lok easier and quicker when you enjoy what you are working on. 
10. Make lists and draw out assignments when working with teammates. Everyone has their own ideas in their head. If we don't show them to others, then we run the risk of miscommunication. It could be a small issue, or it could be a huge issue that will take everyone a lot of time to fix. 


## Recommendations for Future Students

1. Learn to manage your time for this class and all your classes combined. It sounds like they are the same thing, they are not. 
2. Be thorough when creating your curcuits. One wrong move can make or break your board and it is easier to change that electronically than when you already have your PCB. 
3. Have fun when creating your projects. This is the time you are puting your designs in your mind into reality. 
4. Ask questions whenever you feel confused. If you don't speak up, you'll stay confused or use precious time to research it for hours. If your are learning it, ask then, it will save you time. 
5. Go to office hours. There are many people ther to help and your issue might have been the last issue someone else just solved.
